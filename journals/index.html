My Website is a Shifting House Next to a River. What Could Yours Be? by Laurel Schwulst

Nowadays, we're so used to webistes having a specific mold to fit in to that we've forgotten the lengths you can take your websites to. It can be reflective of the person who made it and act as a forms of expression. I liked the description that websites are like living beings, always changing and inherently incomplete. If a website is truly completed then it's basically dead. Comparing websites to a room is a really good visual because it illustrated the infinite possibilities of what it can contain. Rooms can be an extension of out personalities based on how we choose to decoarate it and that makes it a very personalized space.


A Rant About Technology by Ursula K Le Guin

This provides a very interesting point of view about technology. We're so used to technology being a computers, phones, TVs, or other hi-tech gadgets. Technology is something that's become an ingrained part of all of out lives and it's your connection to the material world. It's not something impossible to learn. Anything that we manage to learn how to make is considered technology. To be honest, I'm still hvaing trouble grasping the this idea of what technology really is because of the society we're living in now. However, this article forces you to think beyond the threshold that we've set for ourselves and expands the horizons of what we're truly capable of doing.

A Handmade Web by J.R. Carpenter

It's very clear that the art of making websites from scratch has dwindled over the years. A lot of websites are made for commercial use or advertisement, and they're made for the visitor's convenice and monetize off of them too. There are so many website builders that people use to make more websites that fit the same mold and its become hard to escape that if you don't know how to make a website. I like the correlation that Carpenter makes between handmade website and print material. It's hard to imagine there being a connection between the two because we've become so used to them being on opposite ends of the spectrum. They can both be personal spaces, works that can feel scrappy and ever changing. During a time where corporations have taken over the web, finding personal, handmade wesites is like finding gold.

They build your algorithm based on that and find ways to tagret you as a user for their own benefit. The apps that I'm seeing in the most (that's infuriating) is TikTok. The Good Room by Frank Chimero

The web has lost the community vibe to it. A lot of websites and apps (such as Instagram, X, and Facebook) advertise themselves as a community based platform but a second later want you to pay or install something if you want more goodies. If the web acted as a library, that would be the dream because there wouldn't be anyone after you trying to grab at your wallet at every turn. Because of this, I'm seeing more and more people doing a "digital detox" by limiting their screentime or even changing to a device that makes it harder to be on social media. It's very obvious how toxic the web has gotten and it seems like an increasingly smarter choice to keep as much distance as you can from it.
